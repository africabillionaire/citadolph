# Cognitive Biases & Heuristics Index

Load this file when the user requests a catalog of cognitive biases, heuristic details, research statistics, or wants to understand how specific biases affect UX decision-making.

---

## 5.1 Banner Blindness
Users ignore ad-like elements and regions traditionally used for advertising.
- **Implementation:** Do not place critical CTAs in banner positions. Make content look like content. Use native advertising and inline recommendations.
- **Impact:** 86% of users exhibit banner blindness (NNGroup). Display banner CTR averages 0.05–0.10%.

## 5.2 Change Blindness
Users miss changes in a scene, especially during brief interruptions (page reload, scroll, transition).
- **Mitigation:** Limit simultaneous changes. Keep changes near focal point. Use animated transitions. Avoid page reloads when possible.
- **Impact:** Change blindness occurs with interruptions as short as 67 ms. Users miss error messages placed away from focus 40–60% of the time.

## 5.3 Inattentional Blindness
Users miss stimuli in plain sight when attention is focused elsewhere (Simons & Chabris, 1999 — gorilla suit study: ~50% miss rate).
- **Mitigation:** Use strategic contrast, persistent notifications, subtle animations. Critical info must be impossible to miss. Use multiple modalities for critical alerts.
- **Impact:** Users focus on ~20–30% of screen content during typical tasks. Critical alerts outside focal area missed by 40–60%.

## 5.4 Negativity Bias
One bad experience outweighs many good ones. Evolutionary: threats were more dangerous than opportunities.
- **Mitigation:** Follow standards. Design error states with same care as success states. Write courteous, helpful error messages. Sprinkle delightful encounters as insurance.
- **Impact:** Negative experiences remembered 2–3× more vividly. One negative interaction can negate 3–5 positive ones. Users encountering errors are 60–80% less likely to return.

## 5.5 Halo Effect
First impressions color all subsequent judgments.
- **Implementation:** Invest in visual design and initial load experience. Maintain brand consistency.
- **Impact:** Users form judgments within 50 ms. 94% of first impressions are design-related. Positive first impressions increase task persistence by 20–30% even when problems occur.

## 5.6 Choice Overload
Too many options lead to buyer's remorse and analysis paralysis (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000: 24 jams → 3% purchase; 6 jams → 30% purchase).
- **Mitigation:** Reduce offerings. Categorize meaningfully. Highlight top products. Use comparative tables (≤5 items). Smart defaults.
- **Impact:** Amazon's recommendation engine drives 35% of purchases by reducing choice overload. Optimal navigation items: 5–7 top-level categories.

## 5.7 Confirmation Bias
Searching for, interpreting, and remembering information that confirms pre-existing beliefs.
- **Mitigation:** Approach problems from multiple perspectives. Don't ignore challenging information. Watch what users do, not just what they say. Keep findings rooted in data.
- **Impact:** UX practitioners missing confirmation bias miss 30–40% of usability issues. Affects 60–80% of design reviews without structured processes.

## 5.8 Availability Heuristic
Drawing conclusions based on what comes to mind immediately (recent, vivid, emotionally charged events).
- **Implementation:** Design for first impressions. Surface products on relevant search results. Make important content easily findable. Recent reviews influence purchase decisions 3× more than older reviews.
- **Impact:** Recent/vivid information weighted 2–5× more heavily than statistical base rates.

## 5.9 Anchoring
Initial information anchors all subsequent judgment, whether relevant or not (Tversky & Kahneman).
- **Implementation:** Offer good defaults. Show original prices. Use suggested values. Time estimates anchor perceived duration. Show premium tier first.
- **Research:** Tversky & Kahneman — ascending sequence estimate: 512; descending: 2,250 (actual: 40,320). Ariely's SSN study: higher SSN digits = higher bids.
- **Impact:** Anchoring shifted median estimates by 4×. Showing original prices increases perceived value by 20–40%. Suggested donation values increased completions by 23% (Oxfam).

## 5.10 Overconfidence Effect
Overestimating knowledge and abilities.
- **Mitigation:** Run usability tests early and often. Use structured decision-making. Build diverse teams. Document decisions and revisit assumptions.
- **Impact:** Affects ~80–90% of design decisions when not validated.

## 5.11 Framing Bias
Same data, different conclusions based on positive/negative framing.
- **Mitigation:** Consider both frames. Test different framings. Use A/B testing.
- **Impact:** Framing effects can shift preferences by 20–40%.

## 5.12 Status Quo Bias
Resistance to change; preference for current state.
- **Mitigation:** Always question the status quo. Make switching easy with clear benefits. Maintain familiar patterns. Make defaults work for users.
- **Impact:** Users are 2–3× more likely to keep existing defaults than change them.

## 5.13 Common Knowledge Effect
Teams focus on what everyone knows and overlook unique individual insights. ~70–80% of information shared in groups is already known to all members.
- **Mitigation:** Use individual interviews before group discussions. Implement structured processes (brainwriting before brainstorming). Advocate for unshared information.
- **Impact:** Structured processes increase use of unshared information by 30–50%.

## 5.14 Hawthorne Effect
People change behavior when observed.
- **Mitigation:** Create judgment-free settings. Ensure confidentiality. Use realistic tasks. Minimize observer influence. Build rapport over 60–90 min sessions.
- **Impact:** Observed participants may be 15–25% more persistent. Rapport over 60+ minutes significantly reduces behavioral modification.

## 5.15 Survey Bias
- **Acquiescence Bias:** Tendency to agree/check "yes."
- **Social Desirability Bias:** Over-reporting desirable behaviors.
- **Recent Bias:** Giving more weight to recent experiences.
- **Mitigation:** Use direct and indirect questioning. Randomize option order. Include reverse-coded questions. Keep surveys short. Triangulate with behavioral observation.
- **Impact:** Social desirability bias can inflate positive responses by 20–40%. Acquiescence bias causes 15–25% to agree with contradictory statements.
